A · What the game taught

Growth was easy. Why?

  • Public demand +10 % bought +2.4 % of GDP and −1 point of unemployment at once, for −1.1 point on the budget and −0.7 on trade. In the objective function that is a gain.
  • Inflation moved by +0.7 point — and changed nothing afterwards: a thermometer, not a force.
  • The only brake was the common stock of reserves: three months of imports, drained slowly by each member’s leak. With everybody stimulating, several years before the alert.
  • Austerity never paid in R; it only paid in reserves — which belong to everyone.
Diagnosis

In class 6 the union was a closed economy with a leak. Nothing a country did to its prices ever came back to it.

What a real union does to a stimulating member

  • Its prices rise faster than its partners’.
  • Its goods become more costly — inside the union and outside.
  • It exports less, imports more: the trade deficit widens year after year.
  • Without an exchange rate of its own, the only way back is to inflate less than the others: internal devaluation.

B · Version 1 · Trade

The price level enters trade

\( P^{rel}_{i} = P^{rel}_{i,-1}\,\frac{1+\pi_i/100}{1+\pi^*/100} \)
\( IMU_{ij} = (1-s_R)\,\mu\,Y_i\; s_{ij}\left(\frac{P^{rel}_{j,-1}}{P^{rel}_{i,-1}}\right)^{-0.7} \)
\( IMR_i = s_R\,\mu\,Y_i\left(\frac{xr_{-1}}{P^{rel}_{i,-1}}\right)^{-0.7},\qquad XR_i = xR^*_i\,(1+world)\left(\frac{xr}{P^{rel}_{i,-1}}\right)^{0.7} \)
  • \(P^{rel}\): your price level relative to the reference path — everybody, the rest of the world included, inflates at \(\pi^* = 2\) %; only the excess counts. At the reference, \(P^{rel} = 1\) everywhere and nothing moves.
  • Inflation is still the class-6 thermometer, without inertia; what is new is that its level accumulates and reallocates trade with a one-year lag.
  • Elasticities 0.7 inside and outside the union; every identity of class 6 still holds, and the reserves are the same three months of imports.

Notation

\(IMU_{ij}\): imports of \(i\) from partner \(j\) (volume); \(s_{ij}\) its reference share

\(IMR_i\), \(XR_i\): imports from / exports to the rest of the world

\(xr\): the union’s fixed exchange rate; \(\pi^*\): reference inflation, 2 %

Everything else — households, firms, banks, government, central bank — is class 6, unchanged.

B · Version 1 · Trade

A lone stimulus now erodes — and leaves a deficit behind

France, public demand +10 % for six years

  • GDP gain: +2.4 % in year 1, +1.2 % in year 6 (class 6: it kept rising to +2.6 %).
  • Trade balance: −0.7 point in year 1, −1.4 in year 6 (class 6: −0.7 throughout).
  • Price level: +0.5 % above the union after one year, +2.2 % after six.
  • Partners gain a little from France’s imports (+0.14 % of GDP for Germany) — and keep gaining (+0.22 %) as France loses competitiveness.

Stop the stimulus after three years: GDP falls below the reference (−0.8 %) because the prices do not come back by themselves.

B · Version 1 · Trade

What changes on your page — and in your strategy

On the page

  • A new line in your results: price level vs the union — your competitiveness, in %.
  • Every result is split in three: your own policy, the other governments, the shocks. Your analysis has to use it.
  • A chart, « Your objective, year by year »; the board shows every country side by side.
  • The desk is in English or French, with a beginner’s guide on every lever and vote; the model page has every equation.

In your strategy

  • A stimulus still pays in year 1 — and costs more every year after. Ask: for how long?
  • Your inflation is now your competitiveness: the consumption-tax lever moves both.
  • Watch your partners’ prices: their inflation is your export market.
  • The crisis vote will come; decide beforehand what you want — and line up the votes at the council.
B · Version 1 · Trade

Reading the chart

  • Each bar is one year. Above zero: what helped your score (GDP up, unemployment down…); below zero: what hurt it. Each segment is weight × deviation, so the segments add up exactly to R.
  • The dashed line is R, year by year — the ranking sums it over the years.
  • Here: a stimulus. GDP and employment carry the score in year 1; the trade balance eats more of it each year, and R drifts below zero by year 3. That drift is the price channel.
C · Version 2 · Finance

So far, nothing but goods crossed the union’s border

  • The rest of the world bought the union’s exports and sold it imports — and held none of its assets. The union held none of theirs.
  • So the balance of payments had a current account and reserves, nothing else: no capital account, no portfolio choice, no interest-rate parity.
  • Godley & Lavoie’s chapter 12 adds exactly that: households of each economy hold both countries’ bills; the exchange rate is fixed if reserves absorb, floating if they do not.

The Finance model — what is added to Trade

  • The rest of the world holds union government bills, 15 % of the union’s GDP; union households hold foreign bills, about 7 % of their wealth.
  • Both portfolios move with the interest-rate differential \(r - r_w\) (static exchange-rate expectations).
  • The balance of payments gains a financial account: \(CA' + KA = xr\cdot\Delta R\).
  • A regime set by the administrator — fixed or floating — kept from one year to the next until it is changed.
C · Version 2 · Finance

Fixed or floating: the same equation, a different unknown

Fixed exchange rate

\( xr \text{ given} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \Delta R = \frac{CA' + KA}{xr} \)
  • Reserves absorb whatever trade and capital do not balance.
  • The rate vote is a reserves decision: +0.5 point draws capital in, reserves +25 %; union GDP −0.6 % the next year.

Floating exchange rate

\( R \text{ given} \quad\Rightarrow\quad CA'(xr) + KA(xr) = 0 \)
  • No reserves crisis: the currency moves instead — depreciation raises import prices, then restores exports.
  • The rate vote is a currency decision: +0.5 point → inflows → appreciation of 3.7 % → inflation −0.5 point, union GDP −0.9 % the next year.

Mundell–Fleming, written with stocks: the regime decides whether the adjustment falls on the central bank’s balance sheet or on the price of the currency.

C · Version 2 · Finance

The bond market looks at your debt; the central bank decides whether to stand behind you

\( s_i = \varphi \cdot \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{B_{i,-1}}{Y_{i,-1}} - 1.10\right), \quad \varphi = 5 \)
  • Above 110 % of last year’s GDP, a member pays a spread — 1 point at 130 % — to its households, its banks, and the rest of the world. The part paid abroad is a leak in the current account; the part paid at home is income.
  • France, public demand and benefits +10 % for eight years: debt 104 → 123 %, a spread from year 5, 0.5 point in year 8; public balance −2.9 points (Trade model: −2.8).
  • Foreign investors flee high-spread members: their bills are sold, the union’s balance of payments feels it — reserves in a fixed regime, the currency in a floating one. One member’s debt is everybody’s external constraint.
  • The common central bank buys the bills nobody wants, at the common rate: the backstop that keeps spreads contained (OMT 2012, TPI 2022).

Three new administrator shocks

  • Sudden stop: the rest of the world cuts its holdings of union bills by x %. Fixed: −5 % costs a fifth of the reserves, −20 % puts them under the alert at once. Floating: −20 % → the currency drops 13 %, inflation +1.7 points, then the trade balance improves.
  • World interest rate: capital leaves when \(r_w\) rises above the union’s rate.
  • Backstop off: same French stimulus, spread 1.3 points in year 8, public balance −3.2 — Greece 2010, Italy 2011.
C · Version 2 · Finance

Three votes at the council

The central bank’s rate

cutholdhike

Every year, with your decisions. ±0.5 point, permanent. Majority of the countries that vote.

Fixed: a reserves decision. Floating: a currency decision.

A reserves crisis

austeritydevaluationfloat

When the reserves ended the year under the alert, before the next decisions. The choices lie on a line; the median vote wins.

Austerity caps public demand and benefits at −5 %; devaluation 10 %; float only exists in Finance.

A currency crisis

defendlet it float

Floating regime, depreciation of more than 10 % in a year. Defend = rate +1 point.

Spend output to save the currency, or let import prices do the adjusting.

Votes are secret until the year is run. Pledges made at the council are recorded — and compared with the votes. The administrator may also hold an election: the weights of R change for a country or the whole union, from a given year.
