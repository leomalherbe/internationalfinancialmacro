B · What the game taught

Growth was easy. Why?

  • Public demand +10 % bought +2.4 % of GDP and −1 point of unemployment at once, for −1.1 point on the budget and −0.7 on trade. In the objective function that is a gain.
  • Inflation moved by +0.7 point — and changed nothing afterwards: a thermometer, not a force.
  • The only brake was the common stock of reserves: three months of imports, drained at ~15 % of a stimulus’s leak. With everybody stimulating, two to three years before the alert.
  • Austerity never paid in R; it only paid in reserves — which belong to everyone.
Diagnosis

In class 6 the union was a closed economy with a leak. Nothing a country did to its prices ever came back to it.

What a real union does to a stimulating member

  • Its prices rise faster than its partners’ (Phillips).
  • Its goods become dearer — inside the union and outside.
  • It exports less, imports more: the trade deficit widens year after year.
  • Without an exchange rate of its own, the only way back is to inflate less than the others: internal devaluation.

Germany 2000–2010, Southern Europe 2010–2015.

C · The missing channel

Version 1.2: the price level enters trade

\( P^{rel}_{i} = P^{rel}_{i,-1}\,\frac{1+\pi_i/100}{1+\pi^*/100} \)
\( IMU_{ij} = (1-s_R)\,\mu\,Y_i\; s_{ij}\left(\frac{P^{rel}_{j,-1}}{P^{rel}_{i,-1}}\right)^{-0.7} \)
\( IMR_i = s_R\,\mu\,Y_i\left(\frac{xr_{-1}}{P^{rel}_{i,-1}}\right)^{-0.7},\qquad XR_i = xR^*_i\,(1+world)\left(\frac{xr}{P^{rel}_{i,-1}}\right)^{0.7} \)
  • \(P^{rel}\): your price level relative to the reference path — everybody, the rest of the world included, inflates at \(\pi^* = 2\) %; only the excess counts. At the reference, \(P^{rel} = 1\) everywhere and nothing moves.
  • Inflation is still the class-6 thermometer, without inertia; what is new is that its level accumulates and reallocates trade with a one-year lag.
  • Elasticities 0.7 inside and outside the union; every identity of class 6 still holds.

Notation

\(IMU_{ij}\): imports of \(i\) from partner \(j\) (volume); \(s_{ij}\) its reference share

\(IMR_i\), \(XR_i\): imports from / exports to the rest of the world

\(xr\): the union’s fixed exchange rate; \(\pi^*\): reference inflation, 2 %

Everything else — households, firms, banks, government, central bank — is class 6, unchanged.

C · The missing channel

A lone stimulus now erodes — and leaves a deficit behind

France, public demand +10 % for six years

  • GDP gain: +2.4 % in year 1, +1.2 % in year 6 (class 6: it kept rising to +2.6 %).
  • Trade balance: −0.7 point in year 1, −1.4 in year 6 (class 6: −0.7 throughout).
  • Price level: +0.5 % above the union after one year, +2.2 % after six.
  • Partners gain a little from France’s imports (+0.2 % of GDP) — and keep gaining as France loses competitiveness.

Stop the stimulus after three years: GDP falls below the reference (−0.8 %) because the prices do not come back by themselves.

C · The missing channel

Internal devaluation: the mirror image

  • France cuts public demand by 10 % for six years: GDP −2.4 % then −1.2 %; the trade balance improves from +0.7 to +1.5 points; prices 2 % below the union’s.
  • The cost is paid first (output, unemployment), the gain comes slowly (competitiveness) — and stays: once prices are lower, the surplus persists after the austerity ends.
  • Inside a union there is no other way to regain competitiveness: no exchange rate, no tariffs. This is the adjustment the euro area imposed on Greece, Spain and Portugal after 2010.

Hume’s mechanism, in a union

A country that stimulates inflates, loses competitiveness, runs a deficit; its partners gain the demand and the surplus. Relative prices do the adjustment that the exchange rate cannot — slowly, and at the expense of output. David Hume described it for gold in 1752; a monetary union is a gold standard with a central bank.

Symmetric stimulus by all members: no change in relative prices inside the union, no reallocation — only the deficit with the rest of the world.

C · The missing channel

Smaller reserves: the alert comes in two years, not five

Reserves of the union, % of the initial stock, no reaction

  • Initial stock: 1.5 months of imports from the rest of the world (class 6: three).
  • World demand −5 % every year: 69 → 40 → 15 % — alert at the end of year 3, exhausted in year 4.
  • World demand −10 %: 37 % after one year, exhausted in year 2 — straight to the forced devaluation, no vote.
  • Every member stimulating by 5 %: 88 → 73 → 54 %, with no shock at all.

Same rules as before: alert at 25 %, crisis vote before the decisions of the next year, forced devaluation of 15 % when the stock is gone and the deficit continues.

D · Playing version 1.2

What changes on your page — and in your strategy

On the page

  • A new line in your results: price level vs the union — your competitiveness, in %.
  • A chart, « Your objective, year by year »: each year, the contributions of the five dimensions to R stacked above and below zero, and R itself as a dashed line. The board shows the seven countries side by side.
  • The model page (equations, notation, orders of magnitude) is updated: read it before deciding.

In your strategy

  • A stimulus still pays in year 1 — and costs more every year after. Ask: for how long?
  • Your inflation is now your competitiveness: the VAT lever moves both.
  • Watch your partners’ prices: their inflation is your export market.
  • The reserves run out twice as fast. The crisis vote will come; decide beforehand whether you want austerity or a devaluation — and line up the votes at the council.

Same four levers, same objective, same two votes. The union you govern is simply less forgiving.

D · Playing version 1.2

Reading the new chart

  • Each bar is one year. Above zero: what helped your score (GDP up, unemployment down…); below zero: what hurt it. Each segment is weight × deviation, so the segments add up exactly to R.
  • The dashed line is R, year by year — the ranking sums it over the years.
  • Here: a stimulus. GDP and employment carry the score in year 1; the trade balance eats more of it each year, and R drifts below zero by year 3. That drift is the new channel.
D · Opening the capital account

So far, nothing but goods crossed the union’s border

  • The rest of the world bought the union’s exports and sold it imports — and held none of its assets. The union held none of theirs.
  • So the balance of payments had a current account and reserves, nothing else: no capital account, no portfolio choice, no interest-rate parity.
  • Class 4 told you where that fails: capital flows are larger than trade flows, and they move on returns, not on goods.
  • Godley & Lavoie’s chapter 12 adds exactly that: households of each economy hold both countries’ bills; the exchange rate is fixed if reserves absorb, floating if they do not.

Version 1.3 — what is added

  • The rest of the world holds union government bills, 30 % of the union’s GDP; union households hold foreign bills, 15 % of their wealth.
  • Both portfolios move with the interest-rate differential \(r - r_w\) (static exchange-rate expectations).
  • The balance of payments gains a financial account: \(CA' + KA = xr\cdot\Delta R\).
  • A per-year regime chosen by the administrator: fixed or floating — switchable during the game.
D · Opening the capital account

Fixed or floating: the same equation, a different unknown

Fixed exchange rate

\( xr \text{ given} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \Delta R = \frac{CA' + KA}{xr} \)
  • Reserves absorb whatever trade and capital do not balance.
  • A sudden stop drains them; the alert and the crisis vote work as before.
  • The rate vote is a reserves decision: a hike attracts capital and slows the drain.

Floating exchange rate

\( R \text{ given} \quad\Rightarrow\quad CA'(xr) + KA(xr) = 0 \)
  • No reserves crisis: the currency moves instead — depreciation raises import prices and inflation for everyone, then restores exports.
  • The rate vote is a currency decision: hike → inflows → appreciation → lost competitiveness, cheaper imports.
  • A depreciation of more than 10 % in a year opens a vote: defend (rate +1 pt) or let it float.

Mundell–Fleming, written with stocks: the regime decides whether the adjustment falls on the central bank’s balance sheet or on the price of the currency.

D · Opening the capital account

The bond market looks at your debt — and the central bank decides whether to stand behind you

\( s_i = \varphi \cdot \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{B_i}{Y_i} - 1.10\right), \qquad \varphi = 5 \;\;(\text{1 point at 130\% of GDP}) \)
  • Above 110 % of GDP, a member pays a spread on its bills — to its households, its banks, and the rest of the world. The part paid abroad is a leak in the current account; the part paid at home is income.
  • Foreign investors flee high-spread members: their bills are sold, the union’s balance of payments feels it — reserves in a fixed regime, the currency in a floating one. One member’s debt is everybody’s external constraint.
  • The common central bank buys the bills nobody wants, at the common rate: that is the backstop that keeps spreads contained (OMT 2012, TPI 2022).

Two new administrator shocks

  • Sudden stop: the rest of the world cuts its holdings of union bills by x %. Fixed: the reserves pay. Floating: the currency drops.
  • Backstop off: the central bank stops absorbing the bills of members above the threshold. Their spread applies to their whole debt, the flight is no longer cushioned — Greece 2010, Italy 2011.

Both are announced in the communiqué and replayed year by year like the other shocks.
