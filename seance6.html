A · What OPEN-MU is

A monetary union built on the model you already know

  • A monetary union of several economies, each running the open-economy stock-flow consistent model of class 5 (Godley & Lavoie, chapter 6): households, a government, trade with the partners.
  • One currency, one central bank, one stock of foreign reserves; a passive rest of the world that buys from and sells to the union at a fixed exchange rate.
  • Every result is a deviation from the stationary reference: what changes because of what you — and the others — did. Not a forecast.
  • The game follows the spirit of MacSim 2 (Brillet, Cette, Gambini & Lagoarde-Ségot, Politiques macroéconomiques : un outil d’évaluation, De Boeck Supérieur, 2014), with a model you already know.

Why a game?

Each team is a government. You choose policies, the model computes the consequences — for you and for everybody else — and one number, the objective function, says how well you did.

The mechanisms of classes 1–5 act together, in a union where the other governments move too.

Stress "deviation from the stationary reference": students will read tables of +2.5 % and −1.0 pt, not levels. The reference is the same for everyone; the administrator can disturb it with shocks.

A · The objective

One number to maximise: the objective function

\( R_{i,t} \;=\; a_1\,\Delta \text{GDP}_{i,t} \;-\; a_2\,\Delta U_{i,t} \;-\; a_3\,\Delta \pi_{i,t} \;+\; a_4\,\Delta \text{PB}_{i,t} \;+\; a_5\,\Delta \text{TB}_{i,t} \)
+GDPvolume, gap in %
−Unemploymentrate, gap in points
−Inflationrate, gap in points
+Public balancegap in points of GDP
+Trade balancegap in points of GDP

Δ = deviation from the stationary reference, for that country and that year. The weights \(a_i\) are set by the administrator; the desk computes \(R\) for every country every year.

Your objective: maximise \(R\)

The five components are the classic objectives of macro policy (the "magic square" plus the public balance). Insist on the signs: unemployment and inflation enter negatively.
A · The objective

Why the five objectives fight each other

A demand stimulus (spending ↑, benefits ↑, income tax ↓)

demand→GDP→unemployment
…butimportsinflationpublic balance

GDP and jobs improve; the trade balance, the budget and inflation pay.

A consumption-tax rise

tax receipts→public balanceandimports
…butprices, oncedemandunemployment

The budget and the trade balance improve; output, jobs and prices pay.

Key idea

Every lever moves several objectives, with opposite signs — that is why the game is a game

A good policy is a mix that targets each objective with the instrument that hurts the others least, and a timing that lets the effects arrive before the costs accumulate.

A · The instruments

Four levers

Public spending
Public demand \(G\)public purchases of goods and services — 20 % of GDP at the reference
Social benefits \(TR\)transfers to households (pensions, allowances, insurance) — 15 % of GDP
Taxes
Household income tax rate \(\theta\)on income and interest received
Consumption tax rate \(\tau\)a VAT: taken on household spending — 10 % at the reference

How a value is read

Each value is a relative variation, in %, of the country’s own reference level, for that year. Consumption tax +5 means the rate rises by 5 % of its level (from 10 % to 10.5 %), not by 5 points.

0 = no change from the reference. A measure kept for three years is entered three times.

Bounds in our game: ±10 % per lever.

A · The shocks

And the administrator holds the dice

Shocks on the whole union

  • World demand: the rest of the world buys more, or less, from everyone — each member hit according to its exports.
  • Central-bank rate: one rate for the union — it cuts firms’ investment with a one-year lag (contractionary) and raises the debt service of every government.
  • Household confidence: the propensity to consume out of income moves up or down.
  • Import propensity (an oil-type shock): more is spent abroad for the same output.

The administrator’s last resort

  • Devaluation, decreed when the union’s reserves approach the alert line: import prices up at once, exports up with a lag.

How you will learn about them

Through the administrator’s communiqué on your page, at the start of each round — and in your results, where the report separates the effects of the shocks from the effects of your policy and of the other members’ policies. The board shows the union’s reserves.

Shocks are what a government actually faces: you do not choose the storm, you choose the sails.

A · The rhythm

How the session unfolds

1

Warm-up

Every group governs France for three years. The other members do nothing. A plan is submitted in one go, with a written strategy; the report comes back; you explain what happened.

2

The game, year by year

One member per group. The administrator announces the year’s shocks; you decide for the year; the simulation runs; everybody reads the results and the ranking; next year.

A · The report

What comes back after each run

For your country and year: the five deviations from the stationary reference — GDP, unemployment, inflation, public balance, trade balance — and the value of \(R\). Each of them is split in three:

1

Effects of the shocks

The administrator’s doing: what would have happened to your economy with no policy at all, at home or abroad.

2

Your own policy

What you would have obtained in a union where nobody else moved.

3

Foreign policies

What the other governments did to you, through trade and the common reserves. Zero in the warm-up — the whole point of the real game.

B · Who holds what

The balance sheet of the union

Households hold their wealth \(V_i\) in cash, deposits and the bills of their own government. Firms own the capital and owe it to the banks, which created the deposits by lending; a bank short of deposits borrows from the central bank. The common central bank issues the cash, refinances the banks, buys the bills nobody else wants, and holds the union’s reserves \(R\).

Every financial row sums to zero: a claim for someone is a liability for someone else. Only real capital is net wealth for the union.

Notation

\(i\): a member · \(H_i\): cash · \(M_i\): deposits · \(L_i\): bank loans to firms · \(K_i\): capital stock · \(B_{h,i}\), \(B_{b,i}\), \(B_{cb,i}\): bills held by households, banks, the central bank · \(B_i\): bills issued · \(A_i\): central-bank advances to banks

\(R\): reserves, in world currency · \(xr\): price of one unit of world currency in the union’s currency (\(xr\) up = devaluation) · \(V_i\): household wealth · \(NW_{cb}\): central-bank net worth (valuation gains on reserves only)

B · Demand

Households spend out of income and wealth — class 5, again

\( Y_i = C_i + G_i + I_i + X_i - IM_i \)
\( CE_i = \alpha_1\, YD_i + \alpha_2\, V_{i,-1}, \qquad C_i = (1-\tau_{c,i})\, CE_i \)
\( V_i = V_{i,-1} + YD_i - CE_i \)
\( B_{h,i} = V_i\,(\lambda_0 + \lambda_1\, r), \qquad M_i = \nu\,(V_i - B_{h,i}), \qquad H_i = V_i - B_{h,i} - M_i \)
  • Output is what is bought: consumption, public demand, firms’ investment, exports — minus what is bought abroad.
  • Households spend a fraction of disposable income (\(\alpha_1 = 0.75\)) plus a fraction of last year’s wealth; what they do not spend becomes wealth, held in bills, deposits or cash — more bills when the interest rate is higher.
  • Part of their spending is the consumption tax; the rest is demand for goods.

Notation

\(Y_i\): output (GDP); \(C_i\): consumption of goods; \(G_i\): public demand; \(I_i\): investment; \(X_i\): exports; \(IM_i\): imports, in the union’s currency

\(CE_i\): household spending, tax included; \(YD_i\): disposable income

\(\tau_{c,i} = \tau_i/(1+\tau_i)\): share of spending taken by the consumption tax

\(V_i\): wealth; \(B_{h,i}\): bills held; \(M_i\): deposits; \(H_i\): cash; \(r\): the central bank’s rate

\(\alpha_1, \alpha_2\): propensities to consume out of income and wealth; \(\lambda_0, \lambda_1, \nu\): portfolio parameters

\(x_{-1}\): last year’s value of \(x\)

B · Firms and banks

Firms borrow to invest; banks lend what is asked

\( I_i = I^*_i\,\big(1 - \iota\,(r_{-1} - r^*)\big), \qquad I_i \ge \tfrac{1}{2} I^*_i \)
\( K_i = K_{i,-1} + I_i - AF_i, \qquad AF_i = \delta\, K^*_i, \qquad L_i = K_i \)
\( YF_i = Y_i - AF_i - r_{-1} L_{i,-1} \)
\( B_{b,i} = \max(0,\, M_i - L_i), \qquad A_i = \max(0,\, L_i - M_i) \)
  • At the reference, investment only replaces worn-out capital: 10 % of GDP, a tenth of a capital stock worth one year of output.
  • A higher policy rate lowers the capital firms want and cuts investment the following year — the contractionary channel of monetary policy — while raising every government’s debt service.
  • Banks accommodate: every loan creates a deposit (endogenous money, class 2), at zero spread; a bank short of deposits is refinanced by the central bank. What firms neither retain nor pay in interest goes to households.

Notation

\(I_i\): investment; \(I^*_i\): its reference level; \(K_i\): capital stock; \(K^*_i\): its reference (\(= Y^*_i\))

\(\delta = 0.10\): depreciation rate; \(AF_i\): amortisation funds retained by firms

\(\iota = 12\): sensitivity of investment to the rate; \(r^*\): the reference rate (3 %)

\(L_i\): bank loans; \(M_i\): deposits; \(B_{b,i}\): bills held by banks; \(A_i\): advances from the central bank

\(YF_i\): income distributed to households by firms

Orders of magnitude: rate +1 pt → investment −12 % and GDP ≈ −1.1 % the next year, for every member; public balance ≈ −1 pt

B · Government and central bank

Taxes, debt, and a central bank that buys the rest

\( T_i = \theta_i\,\big(YF_i + r_{-1} (B_{h,i,-1} + M_{i,-1})\big) + \tau_{c,i}\, CE_i \)
\( YD_i = (1-\theta_i)\,\big(YF_i + r_{-1} (B_{h,i,-1} + M_{i,-1})\big) + TR_i \)
\( B_i = B_{i,-1} + G_i + TR_i + r_{-1} B_{i,-1} - T_i - F_{cb,i} \)
\( B_{cb,i} = B_i - B_{h,i} - B_{b,i} \)
  • The government taxes household income — what firms distribute plus the interest on bills and deposits — at rate \(\theta_i\), and spending at rate \(\tau_{c,i}\); it spends on goods and on benefits, and pays interest on its debt.
  • Whatever the deficit, the bills households and banks do not demand are bought by the central bank: money is endogenous, the interest rate is the policy variable.
  • The central bank’s interest income \(F_{cb,i}\) — on its bills and on its advances to banks — is rebated to the government.

Notation

\(T_i\): tax receipts; \(\theta_i\): income tax rate; \(\tau_{c,i}\): consumption tax share

\(TR_i\): social benefits; \(G_i\): public demand

\(YF_i\): income distributed by firms; \(M_i\): deposits

\(B_i\): government debt (bills); \(B_{h,i}\), \(B_{b,i}\), \(B_{cb,i}\): bills held by households, banks, the central bank

\(F_{cb,i} = r_{-1} (B_{cb,i,-1} + A_{i,-1})\): central-bank profit rebated to government \(i\)

Public balance \(PB_i = T_i + F_{cb,i} - G_i - TR_i - r_{-1} B_{i,-1}\), read in points of GDP

B · Trade

Your imports are their exports — inside the union and beyond

\( IM_i = \mu\, Y_i = IMU_i + xr\cdot IMR_i \)
\( X_i = \sum_j s_{ji}\, IMU_j \;+\; XR_i \)
\( XR_i = \bar{x}_i \cdot \tfrac{Y_{RoW}}{Y_{RoW,0}} \cdot \left(\tfrac{xr}{xr_0}\right)^{\eta}, \qquad IMR_i = s_R\, \mu\, Y_i \left(\tfrac{xr_{-1}}{xr_0}\right)^{-\varepsilon} \)
  • Imports are a fixed share of output (30 %): half from the partners, in shares that follow their sizes, half from the rest of the world. What \(i\) imports from \(j\) is \(j\)’s export.
  • With the rest of the world, prices matter: the world price is 1, so an import costs \(xr\). A devaluation makes exports cheaper abroad at once and imports dearer at once — but import volumes adjust only the year after (the J-curve).

Notation

\(\mu\): import propensity; \(s_{ij}\): share of \(j\) in \(i\)’s intra-union imports; \(s_R\): share going to the rest of the world

\(IMU_j\): intra-union imports of \(j\); \(XR_i\), \(IMR_i\): exports to and imports from the rest of the world (volumes)

\(\bar{x}_i\): reference exports to the rest of the world; \(Y_{RoW}\): world demand (shock)

\(\eta\), \(\varepsilon\): price elasticities of exports and imports (0.7 each)

Trade balance \(TB_i = X_i - IM_i\), read in points of GDP

B · The exchange rate

Fixed rate: the reserves absorb what trade does not balance

\( CA = \sum_i \left( XR_i - xr \cdot IMR_i \right), \qquad \Delta R = CA / xr \)
  • Inside the union, no exchange rate. With the rest of the world, the rate is fixed: when the union imports more than it exports, the central bank sells reserves — \(R\) falls.
  • The reserves are shared: one member’s stimulus leaks into imports, and everybody’s reserves pay for it. Reference stock: three months of the union’s imports from the rest of the world.
  • Alert line at 25 % of the initial stock. Below it, the union must choose — austerity or devaluation — by a vote (part D). Reserves gone while the deficit continues: a forced devaluation.
Twin deficits

At the stationary state households stop saving, so for every member: public deficit = external deficit. A permanent stimulus is a permanent drain on the common reserves.

Notation

\(CA\): current account of the union with the rest of the world, in the union’s currency

\(R\): foreign reserves, in world currency; \(R_0\): the initial stock

\(xr\): fixed exchange rate; a devaluation of 10 % multiplies it by 1.1

Reserves earn no interest; valuation gains on a devaluation stay in the central bank’s net worth.

B · Two thermometers

Unemployment and inflation: read on the model, not inside it

\( gap_i = 100\,\left(\frac{Y_i}{Y^*_i} - 1\right) \)
\( u_i = u^* - \beta\, gap_i \)
\( \pi_i = \pi^* + \kappa\, gap_i + \rho\, \Delta\tau_i + \omega\, \frac{\Delta xr}{xr_{-1}} \)
  • Okun: unemployment falls when output is above its reference (\(\beta = 0.4\): one point of output gap, 0.4 point of unemployment).
  • Phillips: inflation rises with the output gap (\(\kappa = 0.3\)), with a consumption-tax increase (once), and with a devaluation (imported inflation).
  • These two equations are reporting equations, outside the dynamics: they measure, they do not feed back. The model’s own restoring forces are elsewhere — next slide.

Notation

\(Y^*_i\): reference output; \(gap_i\): output gap in %

\(u_i\): unemployment rate (%); \(u^* = 7\)

\(\pi_i\): inflation rate (%); \(\pi^* = 2\)

\(\Delta\tau_i\): change of the consumption-tax rate, in points; \(\rho = 0.6\)

\(\Delta xr / xr_{-1}\): devaluation, in %; \(\omega = 0.15\)

B · What to expect

What a stimulus buys, what it costs, and what brings it back

A rise in public demand

  • Output rises with a multiplier of about 1.2 at impact: income → consumption → income.
  • Thirty cents of every extra euro leak into imports: your trade balance worsens, your partners’ exports rise — every deficit is someone else’s surplus.
  • The budget pays: spending up, taxes recover only part of it.
  • The union pays too: half the leak goes to the rest of the world, out of the shared reserves.

The restoring forces — and the missing one

  • The wealth target: households save until wealth matches income, then stop — the stimulus settles at a level instead of growing.
  • Taxes: higher income means higher receipts, part of the deficit closes itself.
  • Imports: part of the extra demand never reaches domestic output.
  • No price force: prices are read, not played; only the reserves — and the administrator — set a limit.
A rate rise

Nothing in year 1; in year 2, investment −12 % and GDP ≈ −1 % for everyone in the union, unemployment up, while every budget pays the higher debt service. One rate, seven economies.

Check

France raises public demand by 10 % of its reference (about 2 points of GDP), alone, in year 1. Which pattern is most likely?

C · The assignment

Govern France for three years — alone

The setting

Every group governs France; the six other members keep their policies unchanged. The administrator has applied the same initial shock to everyone — read the communiqué on your page.

The instruments

A policy for years 1, 2 and 3 with the four levers: public demand, social benefits, household income tax, consumption tax. Each within ±10 % of its reference level.

Before the run: your strategy

150 characters at least: which objective each lever targets, through which mechanism, what you expect for the five components of \(R\). One submission per group.

After the run: your analysis

Your page shows the five deviations, \(R\) and the breakdown. Explain the gaps with your forecast: which restoring force, which leak, which timing you had missed.

Objective

Maximise \(R\) over three years — and predict the sign and the size of each result before you see it

Being right for the right reasons is worth more than a good \(R\). Debrief: all plans and all results side by side.

C · Before you click

Design a policy like an economist, not like a gambler

1

Which objective?

Pick the components of \(R\) you go for, and those you accept to lose. You cannot win all five alone.

2

Which channel?

For each instrument, trace the chain to the objective — and the side chains to the other four (imports, budget, the gap, the reserves).

3

Which timing?

A stimulus acts now and settles; its costs accumulate. A three-year profile is a choice, not three copies of year 1.

D · After the warm-up

One member each, year by year, with everybody else moving

  • Each group receives a country.
  • Each year: the administrator’s communiqué (the shocks), a timer, your decisions for the year with a short justification, the run, the results and the ranking on the board.
  • The four levers are open, within ±10 %.
  • Decisions stay secret while the round is open; once it is closed, everyone sees what everyone did.
  • The union’s reserves are a shared resource: what one member drains, all members lose — and the devaluation that follows hits everyone.
  • Two votes: the central bank’s rate, with your decisions; and — when the reserves are low — austerity or devaluation, before any decision is taken (next slide).

The council of ministers

At some point the council is convened: each government may post a public pledge (“we stimulate by 5 if you do”). Decisions remain private until the year is run; afterwards the board shows who kept their word.

Coordination, free-riding, retaliation: the prisoner’s dilemma.

D · Collective decisions

Two votes at the council — the two closures of a fixed exchange rate

The central bank’s rate — with your decisions

  • Each year, each country votes cut, hold or hike; a majority moves the rate by 0.5 pt, permanently. Secret until the round closes.
  • A higher rate cuts investment the following year and raises every budget’s debt service at once.

The reserves crisis — before your decisions

  • Reserves below the alert line at the end of a year: the next year opens with a vote, before any decision — austerity or devaluation. Result announced, then decisions open.
  • Austerity: public demand and benefits at least 5 % below the reference for everyone — no stimulus that year.
  • Devaluation (10 %): exports recover with a lag; import prices and inflation jump.
  • Reserves exhausted and the deficit still running: forced devaluation of 15 %, no vote.
