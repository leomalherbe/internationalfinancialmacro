A · What MacSim 2 is

A macroeconomic simulator, not a forecasting tool

  • Ten advanced economies, each with the same New-Keynesian model: 50–60 equations, 10–15 of them estimated on quarterly data (error-correction form), the rest accounting identities.
  • Linked by trade (a bilateral trade matrix), by interest rates and by exchange rates; the rest of the world is passive.
  • Every result is a deviation from a reference path: what changes because of what you — and the others — did. Not a forecast.
  • Jean-Louis Brillet, Gilbert Cette, Ian Gambini & Thomas Lagoarde-Ségot, Politiques macroéconomiques : un outil d’évaluation — MacSim2, De Boeck Supérieur, 2014.

Why a game?

Each team is a government. You choose policies, the model computes the consequences — for you and for everybody else — and one number, the objective function, says how well you did.

The mechanisms of classes 1–5 act together, in a world where the other governments move too.

Stress "deviation from reference": students will read tables of +0.11 % and −0.07 pt, not levels. The reference path is the same for everyone; the administrator can disturb it with shocks.

A · The objective

One number to maximise: the objective function

\( R_{i,t} \;=\; a_1\,\Delta \text{GDP}_{i,t} \;-\; a_2\,\Delta U_{i,t} \;-\; a_3\,\Delta \pi_{i,t} \;+\; a_4\,\Delta \text{PB}_{i,t} \;+\; a_5\,\Delta \text{TB}_{i,t} \)
+GDPvolume, gap in %
−Unemploymentrate, gap in points
−Inflationconsumer prices, gap in points
+Public balancegap in points of GDP
+Trade balancegap in points of GDP

Δ = deviation from the reference path, for that country and that year. The weights \(a_i\) are set in the software, which computes \(R\) for every country every year.

Your objective: maximise \(R\)

The five components are the classic objectives of macro policy (the "magic square" plus the public balance). Insist on the signs: unemployment and inflation enter negatively.
A · The objective

Why the five objectives fight each other

A demand stimulus (spending ↑, taxes ↓)

demand→GDP→unemployment
…butimportspricespublic balance

GDP and jobs improve; the trade balance, the budget and inflation pay.

A supply-side move (labour costs ↓)

costs→prices→competitiveness→exports
…butpublic balanceandslow growth

Inflation and trade improve, GDP only gradually; the budget pays for the cut.

Key idea

Every lever moves several objectives, with opposite signs — that is why the game is a game

A good policy is a mix that targets each objective with the instrument that hurts the others least, and a timing that lets the slow effects arrive before the costs accumulate.

How a value is read

Each value is a relative change, in %, from the country’s own reference level, for that year. VAT +5 means the VAT rate rises by 5 % of its level (from 20 % to 21 %), not by 5 points.

0 = back to the reference. A measure kept for three years is entered three times.

Bounds in our game: ±10 % per lever; tariffs ±10 points.

VAT sits on the supply side but hits demand too: higher prices cut real wages and consumption.

A · The shocks

And the administrator holds the dice

Global shocks

  • World demand: the rest of the world buys more, or less, from everyone — each country hit according to its openness.
  • Exchange rate vs the rest of the world: competitiveness and import prices move for all.
  • Terms of trade and world tariffs: the price of what you import, the access to foreign markets.

Country-specific shocks

  • Total or active population, total factor productivity, average wages, the risk premium on sovereign debt.

How you will learn about them

Through the administrator’s communiqué on your page, at the start of each round — and in your results, where the report separates the effects of the shocks from the effects of your policy and of the other countries’ policies.

Shocks are what a government actually faces: you do not choose the storm, you choose the sails.

A · The rhythm

How the session unfolds

1

Warm-up

Every group governs France for three years. The other countries do nothing. A plan is submitted in one go, with a written strategy; the report comes back; you explain what happened.

2

A · The report

What comes back after each run

For your country and year: the five deviations from the reference path — GDP, unemployment, inflation, public balance, trade balance — and the value of \(R\). Each of them is split in three:

1

Effects of the shocks

The administrator’s doing: what would have happened to your economy with no policy at all, at home or abroad.

2

Your own policy

What you would have obtained in a world where nobody else moved.

3

Foreign policies

What the other governments did to you, through trade, prices and interest rates. Zero in the warm-up — the whole point of the real game.

B · Architecture

Five blocks per country, and two optional loops

Solid arrows: relations that are always on. Dashed arrows: the financial options the administrator chooses at the start.

In our game: the short-term interest rate follows a Taylor rule plus a risk premium on public deficits; exchange rates follow purchasing-power parity (next slide).

All ten countries have the same equations; only the estimated coefficients and the trade matrix differ.

B · The two financial options we play with

How interest rates and exchange rates are set

Option 1 · Short-term interest rate: a Taylor rule plus a risk premium

\( r_{ST} = \pi + 0.5\,(\pi - 2\%) + 0.5\,(UT - UT_{LT}) + \text{Premium} \)
\( \text{Premium} = a\,(PB_{LT} - PB), \quad a = 0.1 \)

The central bank sets its rate above inflation when inflation exceeds the 2 % target and when capacity utilisation is above its long-run level. Financial markets add a premium when the public balance is below its long-run value. The long-term rate \(r_{LT}\) is a moving average of short-term rates.

Option 2 · Exchange rate: purchasing-power parity

\( \Delta \lambda_{i,j} = \pi_j - \pi_i \)

Country \(i\)’s currency appreciates against \(j\)’s when \(j\)’s inflation is higher, and depreciates when \(i\)’s inflation is higher: inflation differentials are offset, slowly, by the exchange rate. Inside the euro area: one currency, one short-term rate, set on the average member.

Notation

\(r_{ST}\), \(r_{LT}\): short- and long-term interest rates (%)

\(\pi\): inflation rate of consumer prices (%)

\(UT\): capacity utilisation rate of firms; \(UT_{LT}\): its long-run level

\(PB\): public balance, in points of GDP; \(PB_{LT}\): its long-run level

\(\lambda_{i,j}\): exchange rate of country \(i\)’s currency in terms of \(j\)’s; \(\Delta\lambda > 0\) = appreciation of \(i\)

\(\Delta x\): change of \(x\) over the year

B · Demand (1)

Households: consumption follows disposable income

\( \Delta C = 0.24\,\Delta YD \;-\; 0.40\,\Delta U \;-\; 0.05\,\Delta \pi \)
  • Consumption is a proportion of disposable income: when income grows, consumption grows — by about a quarter of the income change within the year (the rest is adjusted later).
  • The proportion falls when unemployment rises: households set aside precautionary savings.
  • It also falls when inflation rises: households rebuild the real value of their money holdings.

Which levers act here: income tax, employee contributions, social benefits, public wages and public employment — everything that changes \(YD\).

Notation

\(C\): household consumption, in volume

\(YD\): household disposable income, net of taxes and social contributions, in volume

\(U\): unemployment rate (% of the active population)

\(\pi\): inflation rate of consumer prices

\(\Delta x\): change of \(x\) over the year (in % for \(C\) and \(YD\), in points for \(U\) and \(\pi\))

Coefficients: simplified reading of the estimated equations.

B · Demand (2)

Firms: investment follows demand, profits and the cost of capital

\( K^{D} = \text{TFP}\cdot\text{GDP} - 0.65\,CK \)
\( CK = P\,(r_{LT} - \delta + 0.02) \)
\( K = 0.05\,K^{D} + 0.95\,K_{-1} + 0.10\,\text{Profit} \)
\( I = K - K_{-1} + \delta\,K_{-1} \)
  • The desired capital stock rises with demand (accelerator) and falls with the cost of capital, itself rising with the long-term interest rate.
  • The actual stock moves slowly towards the desired one (5 % of the gap per year), faster when profits are high.
  • Investment = change in the capital stock + replacement of worn-out capital.

Notation

\(K\): capital stock; \(K_{-1}\): last year’s stock; \(K^{D}\): desired stock

\(I\): investment, in volume

\(\text{TFP}\): total factor productivity; \(\text{GDP}\): output, in volume

\(CK\): cost of capital; \(P\): price of capital goods; \(\delta\): depreciation rate

\(r_{LT}\): long-term interest rate

\(\text{Profit}\): profit rate = (gross operating surplus − corporate tax) / \(K_{-1}\), minus \(r_{LT}\) + 2 %

B · Supply

Output, employment and unemployment

\( L = \text{TFP}\cdot\text{GDP} \;-\; 0.35\,WC, \qquad WC = (1+\tau)\,W \)
\( U = \frac{\text{POP} - L}{\text{POP}} \)
  • Production is Cobb-Douglas: firms combine capital and labour, and substitute one for the other when their relative cost changes.
  • Employment rises with output (and with productivity, which measures how much output a given workforce can deliver) and falls with the cost of labour: the wage plus the employer’s social contributions.
  • Unemployment is the share of the active population without a job. Public employment adds directly to \(L\).

Notation

\(L\): total employment (private + public)

\(WC\): labour cost per worker; \(W\): gross wage per worker

\(\tau\): employer social contribution rate

\(\text{POP}\): active population (people in work or seeking work)

\(U\): unemployment rate

Which levers act here: employer contributions, public employment, the public/private wage, TFP shocks.

B · Wages and prices

Wages chase prices; prices come from costs and from tensions

\( \Delta W = 0.56\,\pi \;-\; 2\,\Delta U \;+\; \text{residual} \)
\( \pi = \alpha\,\Delta \text{Cost} \;+\; 0.19\,\Delta UT \;+\; \text{residual} \)
  • Wages are indexed on inflation (about half of it within the year) and fall when unemployment rises: the bargaining power of workers depends on the labour market.
  • Prices follow production costs — wages, employer contributions, VAT, import prices — with a country-specific pass-through \(\alpha\), and rise when demand runs ahead of capacity (\(UT\) up).
  • Together: a wage–price loop, damped by unemployment. This is the first restoring force against a demand stimulus.

Notation

\(W\): gross wage per worker; \(\Delta W\): its growth rate

\(\pi\): consumer-price inflation; \(\text{Cost}\): unit production cost

\(UT\): capacity utilisation rate

\(\alpha\): share of a cost increase passed on to prices within the year

B · Trade (1)

Your imports are their exports

\( \Delta M_{i,j} = 1.0\,\Delta \text{GDP}_i - 0.49\,\Delta\!\left(\tfrac{MP_{j}}{DP_{i}}\right) + 0.047\,\Delta UT_i \)
\( X_{j,i} = M_{i,j} \)
  • Imports of country \(i\) from country \(j\) grow one-for-one with \(i\)’s GDP: a stimulus at home is partly spent abroad.
  • They fall when foreign goods become dearer relative to domestic goods (price competitiveness), and rise when domestic firms are saturated and cannot deliver.
  • Every import of \(i\) from \(j\) is an export of \(j\) to \(i\): the trade matrix closes the system — one country’s demand is another’s output.

Notation

\(M_{i,j}\): imports of \(i\) from \(j\), in volume; \(X_{j,i}\): exports of \(j\) to \(i\)

\(\text{GDP}_i\): output of \(i\), in volume

\(MP_j\): price of goods imported from \(j\), in \(i\)’s currency; \(DP_i\): price of domestic goods in \(i\)

\(UT_i\): capacity utilisation rate in \(i\)

\(\Delta x\): change over the year

B · Trade (2)

Export prices: inflation, exchange rates and tariffs

\( \Delta XP_{i,j} = 0.27\,\pi_i + 0.14\,\Delta\lambda_{i,j} + \text{Tariff}_{j,i} \)
\( MP_{j,i} = XP_{i,j} \)
  • The price at which \(i\) sells to \(j\) rises with \(i\)’s inflation and with the appreciation of \(i\)’s currency — both erode competitiveness.
  • A tariff imposed by \(j\) on goods from \(i\) raises their price for buyers in \(j\): less imports from \(i\), but higher import prices in \(j\) — which feed into \(j\)’s costs and inflation.
  • The export price of \(i\) is the import price of \(j\): what one country does to its prices, its partners pay for.

In the euro area the exchange rate is common: competitiveness between members depends on inflation differentials only.

Notation

\(XP_{i,j}\): price of \(i\)’s exports to \(j\); \(MP_{j,i}\): price of \(j\)’s imports from \(i\)

\(\pi_i\): inflation in \(i\)

\(\lambda_{i,j}\): exchange rate of \(i\)’s currency in terms of \(j\)’s; \(\Delta\lambda > 0\) = appreciation

\(\text{Tariff}_{j,i}\): tariff rate of \(j\) on imports from \(i\)

B · Restoring forces

Why policies fade: three tensions, three brakes

Tension indicatorWhere it bitesHorizon
Capacity utilisation \(UT\) (product market)prices ↑, exports ↓, imports ↑transitory
Unemployment vs NAIRU (labour market)wages, prices, external and internal demandmedium to long term
Public balance \(PB\) (in points of GDP)risk premium → interest rates → household and firm demandmedium term
Rule 1

Demand policies do not move the long-run equilibrium

They buy output now; prices, interest rates and imports take it back over the years.

Rule 2

Only supply policies move the NAIRU, productivity or participation

Slower to act, but their effects last.

NAIRU: the unemployment rate at which wage and price inflation are stable — the model’s implicit equilibrium rate for each country.

C · The assignment

Govern France for three years — alone

The setting

Every group governs France; the nine other economies keep their policies unchanged. The administrator has applied the same initial shock to everyone — read the communiqué on your page.

The instruments

A policy for years 1, 2 and 3 among six levers: public employment, public demand, social benefits, income tax, employer contributions, VAT. At most four, each within ±10 % of its reference level.

Before the run: your strategy

150 characters at least: which objective each lever targets, through which mechanism, what you expect for the five components of \(R\). One submission per group.

After the run: your analysis

Your page shows the five deviations, \(R\) and the breakdown. Explain the gaps with your forecast: which restoring force, which leak, which timing you had missed.

Objective

Maximise \(R\) over three years — and predict the sign and the size of each result before you see it

Being right for the right reasons is worth more than a good \(R\). Debrief: all plans and all results side by side.

C · How to submit

Your page: one PIN per group, one submission per group

  1. Open the course site → MacSim 2 · Policy desk; enter your group’s 4-digit PIN (one device submits, the others watch).
  2. Read the administrator’s communiqué: the shock, the time left (a countdown runs).
  3. Fill the three columns, one per year. 0 = no change from the reference; a measure kept for three years is typed three times.
  4. The counter tells you how many instruments you are using; the bounds are checked as you type.
  5. Write the strategy, then Submit.
  6. When your results are published, your page shows the five deviations, \(R\) and their breakdown; then the box for your analysis a posteriori.
C · Before you click

Design a policy like an economist, not like a gambler

1

Which objective?

Pick the components of \(R\) you go for, and those you accept to lose. You cannot win all five alone.

2

Which channel?

For each instrument, trace the chain to the objective — and the side chains to the other four (prices, imports, budget, interest rates).

3

Which timing?

Demand acts now and fades; supply acts late and lasts. A three-year profile is a choice, not three copies of year 1.

D · After the warm-up

One country each, year by year, with everybody else moving

  • Each group receives a country.
  • Each year: the administrator’s communiqué (the shocks), a timer, your decisions for the year with a short justification, the run, the results and the ranking on the board.
  • All eleven levers are open, within ±10 %; leaving or joining the euro and productivity shocks need the administrator’s consent.
  • Decisions stay secret while the round is open; once it is closed, everyone sees what everyone did.

The council of ministers

At some point the council is convened: each government may post a public pledge (“we stimulate by 0.5 if you do”). Decisions remain private until the year is run; afterwards the board shows who kept their word.

Coordination, free-riding, retaliation: the prisoner’s dilemma.
